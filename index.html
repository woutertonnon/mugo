Mugo

Swiss wildcamping rules. Know before you pitch.

Mugo is a map of Switzerland that shows how likely it is that one considerate night in a tent is tolerated at a given spot, if you have not asked anyone first. Tap a spot and the app tells you which rules apply there, and quotes them with a link to each source.

The name comes from Pinus mugo, the mountain pine that grows twisted and low along the treeline, which is where many of the rules change.

A chance, not a permission. Swiss law gives no general right to camp in the wild. It has prohibitions at federal, cantonal and communal level, and outside them an unwritten tolerance. So Mugo gives every spot a percentage, not a yes or no. It is not legal advice. Where a landowner, a sign or a local authority tells you something else, follow them.

What it does

  • One number per spot, 0–100 %. The lowest of the federal, cantonal and communal chances at that spot. Each rule can only lower the number.
  • Federal protected areas. Hunting-ban districts, mires, floodplains, dry grassland and the other federal inventories.
  • Cantonal and communal rules. Taken from the cantons' laws and the communes' own ordinances and nature protection zones. Many communes also answered a written enquiry about it.
  • Ground and treeline. Forest, buildings, farmland, water and whether a spot is above or below the treeline all change the answer.
  • The sources, quoted. Each rule is shown in the original words, with a link to its source. An English machine translation is offered.
  • Works offline. The rules and the chance map are inside the app. Only the background map needs a connection.

Privacy

No account, no tracking, no analytics, no ads. Your location is used only when you tap the locate button, and it never leaves your phone. The full privacy policy has the details.
